    "# 🛒 Superstore Sales Performance Analysis\n",
    "\n",
    "An exploratory data analysis of ~10,000 Superstore orders (2014–2017), examining sales, profit, discount behavior, customer segments, shipping, and time-based trends to understand what actually drives — and drains — the business's performance.\n",
    "\n",
    "---\n",
    "\n",
    "## 📌 Overview\n",
    "\n",
    "Raw totals can lie. A category can look like a top performer purely because it has more orders, not because each order is worth more. This project digs past totals into **margin, discount behavior, and product-level profitability** to find where Superstore is genuinely winning — and where it's quietly losing money.\n",
    "\n",

    "\n",

    "\n",
    "## 🎯 Key Business Questions\n",
    "\n",
    "1. Which categories and sub-categories actually drive sales *and* profit — not just one or the other?\n",
    "2. How does discount behavior erode profitability, and does it differ by category?\n",
    "3. Which customer segments and shipping modes matter most?\n",
    "4. How do sales and profit trend over time — is growth accelerating, steady, or fragile?\n",
    "5. Does a top-selling product always mean a top-profiting product?\n",
    "\n",
    "---\n",
    "\n",
    "## 🔥 Key Findings\n",
    "\n",
    "| # | Finding |\n",
    "|---|---|\n",
    "| 1 | **Technology leads on value, Office Supplies leads on volume.** Technology drives the most revenue on both a total and per-order basis (~$453 avg/order); Office Supplies' comparable total sales are largely a function of order count (6,026 orders vs. Technology's 1,847), not per-order value (~$119 avg). |\n",
    "| 2 | **Furniture has a structural margin problem.** Its profit margin (~0.025) is roughly **7x lower** than Office Supplies or Technology (~0.17 each) — despite having comparable total sales (~$742K vs. ~$719K–$836K). |\n",
    "| 3 | **Discount hurts categories in different ways.** Furniture's losses are frequent but individually modest, spread across a broad 30–60% discount range. Technology's losses are rare but severe — one order at a 70% discount lost over **$6,600**, ~3.5x Furniture's worst loss. |\n",
    "| 4 | **2017's growth came with a margin cost.** Sales grew strongly in 2016–2017, but the growth *rate* slightly decelerated (29.5% → 20.4%). Profit growth actually outpaced sales growth in 2015–2016, but fell behind in 2017 (14.2% vs. 20.4%) — a sign of margin dilution that year. |\n",
    "| 5 | **High sales ≠ high profit at the product level.** Cisco TelePresence ranks #3 by sales (~$22.6K) but is completely absent from the profit top 10 — driven by an extreme 50% average discount. HON Task Chairs shows a similar gap, more likely tied to Furniture's thin category-level margins than to discounting alone. |\n",
    "\n",
    "---\n",
    "\n",
    "## 💡 Recommendations\n",
    "\n",
    "- **Review Furniture's discount policy**, especially in the 30–60% range where losses are most frequent, and investigate whether its costs are structurally thin-margin regardless of discounting.\n",
    "- **Apply category-specific discount controls** — a broad cap for Furniture (frequent, distributed losses) vs. manual review for any Technology order above ~50–60% discount (rare, severe losses).\n",
    "- **Audit individual high-revenue products periodically**, not just categories — a strong seller can still be quietly unprofitable (see Cisco TelePresence).\n",
    "- **Investigate what changed in 2017** before assuming future growth will hold the same margin.\n",
